# Whole-pool composite under any fixed exit is NEGATIVE — the exit is the free variable

The zero-discretion floor (enter 10:00, hold to expiry, collect intrinsic; N=1,316):
mean **+15.6% but median −100%**, 60% expire worthless, WR 28.0%. The positive mean is NOT
robust — excluding the top 10 of 1,316 trades flips it negative; by month Apr +57.5% / May
−1.3% / Jun −0.1% (walk-forward halves +39.5% → −8.3%): April-melt-up fuel, not selection
alpha. Under the live same-day GIGO exit the whole-pool composite is likewise robustly
negative (−2 to −6%/day, WR ~30%).

Two hard consequences: (1) **NEVER publish ITM% or a "floor mean" or a tradeable-ROI Flow
Index** — both backfire on inspection; sell the opportunity surface, not a return. (2) The
opportunity is real but the FIXED exit is the problem — the engine surfaces good contracts
and profit depends on HOW they are traded (discretionary entry/exit). This is the core
product thesis and the reason substrate captures the full opportunity surface so the exit
stays a free variable. Depends on [[option-pnl-not-underlying]] and [[path-calibrated-giveback]].
