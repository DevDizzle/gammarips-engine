====================================================================
P1. THE JUDGE CANNOT SEE THE FLAG IT NEEDS
====================================================================

signal-notifier/main.py, the two-tier slate floor:

  4. FAIL-SOFT FLOOR: if fewer than TOURNEY_MIN survive BOTH floors, restore
     dropped candidates ranked by (print count desc NULLS LAST, live_oi desc
     NULLS LAST) up to TOURNEY_MIN so the tournament never starves to zero.
     Restored rows are marked `_print_floor_restored=True` (internal; popped
     before /rank, surfaced only in the operator email).

"Popped before /rank" is the defect in six words. The flag is computed
correctly, ordered correctly (restores append BELOW survivors), and then
discarded at the one boundary where it would change an outcome.

signal-judge/app/agent.py:152 acknowledges the intent:

  #  the fail-soft restore path deliberately puts sub-floor names back in

Deliberate is fine. Invisible is not. The judge is being handed contaminated
input and told nothing, so it optimizes over the whole slate and lands on
sub-floor names roughly in proportion to how much of the slate they are -- and
on a day when only 2 of 8 clear, that is most of the slate.

Fix, smallest version: keep `_print_floor_restored` in the /rank payload and
have the judge treat it as a hard exclusion when at least one genuine survivor
exists. Restored rows remain available only when NOTHING cleared, and in that
case the card should say so in those words.

====================================================================
P2. LET IT RETURN NOTHING
====================================================================

TOURNEY_MIN=8 is a floor on slate SIZE, and there is no corresponding floor on
slate QUALITY. When the market only offers 2 tradeable names, the engine
manufactures 6 more from the reject pile and ranks all 8 as if they were peers.

Our own screen this morning found 3 of 50 pool names clearing both gates, and
the pool is the same universe the tournament draws from. On a board that thin
the honest slate is 3 names, and the honest pick may be none.

"The tournament never starves to zero" reads like a reliability property. It is
actually a correctness bug: it guarantees an output even when the correct
output is an empty set. A no-pick day is a product feature -- it is the single
most credible thing a signal service can say -- and right now the engine is
structurally incapable of saying it.

Suggested shape: keep TOURNEY_MIN as a soft target, add a hard rule that a
restored row can never become the PICK while any genuine survivor exists, and
emit "NO PICK TODAY -- N of M candidates cleared the liquidity floors" when
zero clear. We will score no-pick days in the funnel log the same as any other.

====================================================================
P3. NOBODY IS MEASURING SPREAD (the actual ask)
====================================================================

This is the one the owner flagged directly and it is the root cause behind all
of the above.

The pipeline gates on open interest and print count. Neither is the thing that
decides whether a subscriber can exit. We have measured the gap repeatedly:

  * 2026-07-31: UNP 08/21 310C carried 2,034 OI and traded ZERO contracts all
    session. OI is not liquidity.
  * 2026-08-05: the spread gate dropped 4 of 11 names that had already passed
    every volume test -- U 24.0%, FIG 24.3%, PATH 15.4%, ANET 12.5%.
  * 2026-08-05: ZETA showed $19,430 on the bid, HOOD $2,681. Same spread
    percentage tier, 7x difference in what you can actually get out of.
  * 2026-08-12 (today): MDB passed OI 399 > OI_FLOOR 200 and still quoted a
    44.6% spread on a 15-lot bid.

get_liquidity serves no bid/ask on the current Polygon plan (GAP-001,
documented since July). That is why the engine gates on proxies: the real
number is not available to it. Every session the harness has to go pull the
Robinhood book separately to answer the question the engine's own liquidity
tool is named after.

Concretely, the two numbers we want on the candidate row and on the card face:

  spread_pct   = (ask - bid) / mark
  bid_depth_usd = bid_size * bid * 100

With those two fields the fail-soft restore becomes safe (restore by real
fillability instead of by print count), the judge's rationale stops claiming
"securely execute" on a 15-lot bid, and the card can carry a number a
subscriber can act on. Without them the engine is guessing, and today it
guessed 44.6%.
